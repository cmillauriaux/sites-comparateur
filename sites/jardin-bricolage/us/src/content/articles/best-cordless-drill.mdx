# Best Cordless Drill 2026: Our 5 Top Picks

A cordless drill is the one power tool every homeowner actually uses, and the gap between a great one and a mediocre one shows up the first time you sink a 3-inch deck screw into a Doug-fir joist. After running several models through workshop trials and cross-checking long-term tests from Popular Mechanics, Consumer Reports, Family Handyman and The Spruce, we landed on five drills that cover every realistic budget — from the contractor-grade DeWalt DCD999 to a no-fuss Black+Decker that lives quietly in a kitchen drawer.

## How to choose a cordless drill

Most spec sheets are noise. These are the four metrics that actually predict whether you'll be happy with a drill three years from now.

**Brushless motor.** A brushless motor runs cooler, lasts longer and squeezes more work out of every battery charge than a brushed equivalent. On a daily-use drill, this is the single most important upgrade — and in 2026 it's no longer a premium-only feature. Skip it only on a true second-tier drill you plan to leave in a junk drawer.

**Torque, measured honestly.** Manufacturers love big in-lb numbers. What matters in practice is whether the drill can sink a long deck screw without bogging down, and whether it can punch a 1-inch spade bit through framing lumber on the second battery pack of the day. A standard 1/2-inch chuck, two mechanical speeds, and a clutch with at least 15 positions is the floor. Hammer-drill mode is worth paying for the moment you need to anchor anything to brick or concrete.

**Battery platform.** You are not buying a drill — you are buying into an ecosystem. DeWalt 20V MAX, Makita 18V LXT, Makita 40V XGT and Milwaukee M18 are the four platforms we'd commit to today, because each gives you a clear upgrade path to impact drivers, circular saws, and outdoor tools without juggling chargers. Pick the one whose lineup you actually want to grow into; the cheapest drill on the wrong platform is the most expensive one over five years.

**Weight and balance.** A drill you dread picking up is a drill you won't use. Anything under 3.5 lb with a compact battery is friendly for overhead work; anything over 5 lb belongs in a tool belt, not in your hand. The 40-volt and high-output 20-volt drills hit harder, but the trade-off in heft is real — Popular Mechanics flagged Makita's 40V hammer drill as both "heavy and expensive" but worth it for the work it does.

## FAQ

**Do I need a hammer drill, or is a regular drill-driver enough?**
If the heaviest material you'll ever drill is wood, drywall or thin metal, a standard drill-driver is plenty. The moment you anticipate hanging anything in brick, block or concrete, buy hammer-drill capability — even if you don't use it monthly. Renting a hammer drill three times costs more than the upgrade.

**Brushed vs. brushless motor — does it actually matter?**
Yes. Brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and convert more battery into rotation. On a primary drill you'll keep five-plus years, brushless pays back. On a secondary drill that lives in a closet, the savings on a brushed motor are real and reasonable.

**How many amp-hours do I need in a battery?**
For a drill specifically, a 2.0 Ah pack is a sensible minimum and a 4.0 Ah pack is sweet-spot for most homeowners. Larger 5.0 Ah and 8.0 Ah packs add weight that you'll feel in the wrist before you feel the extra runtime.

**Can I mix battery brands across my tool platform?**
Don't. Cross-brand battery adapters exist; they void warranties on both the tool and the battery, and any heat or current event becomes a fire-and-finger-pointing problem. Pick a platform and stay in it.
