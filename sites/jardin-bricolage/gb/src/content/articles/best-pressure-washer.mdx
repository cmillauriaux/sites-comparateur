# Best Pressure Washer 2026: Our Tested UK Picks

A British spring usually means a green-streaked patio, a moss-pocked drive and a car that has been quietly ignoring you all winter. The right pressure washer clears the lot in an afternoon, but the wrong one will trip a circuit, kink at the reel and leave you scrubbing by hand anyway. Below are the four machines we recommend for UK households this year, with a clear pick at every budget.

## How to choose a pressure washer

Specs sheets are noisy. These are the four numbers and features we actually weigh up before pressing buy.

**Pressure (bar) and flow rate (L/min) — together, not separately.** Pressure tells you how hard the water hits; flow rate tells you how much of it lands. A 140-bar machine pushing 7 L/min will strip a moss-bound patio noticeably faster than a 150-bar one limping along at 5 L/min. For a typical UK home — patio, drive, car, occasional fence — the sweet spot is roughly 130–145 bar with at least 6 L/min. Anything north of 150 bar is built for builders' yards, and you'll pay for it in weight and noise.

**Motor type: induction beats universal.** Induction motors run quieter, last longer and tolerate continuous use; universal (brushed) motors are lighter and cheaper but whine, wear and overheat on long jobs. If you've a long drive or you'll wash more than a couple of cars a month, pay the premium for induction.

**Hose length and storage.** A 6-metre hose is the practical minimum — anything shorter has you shuffling the unit every five minutes. Equally important is how the hose, lance and cable stow away. A reel and on-board lance clips are not luxuries when the machine lives in a small shed; tangled hose is the single biggest reason pressure washers get abandoned mid-task.

**Lance options and detergent delivery.** A vario (adjustable) lance plus a rotary/turbo nozzle is the bare minimum for a varied UK garden — the vario for cars and timber, the turbo for stone. A patio-cleaner head (the disc with two enclosed jets) is worth the upgrade if you've more than a few square metres of paving; it cuts cleaning time roughly in half and stops the streaking you get from freehand work. Built-in detergent tanks are tidier than the suction-tube alternative; both work, but tanks won't gum up if you forget to flush them.

**Build quality and serviceability.** Brass fittings outlast plastic. A metal pump head outlasts a polymer one. Replaceable seals and a published spares list (Stihl, Nilfisk and Husqvarna all publish theirs) mean a leaky o-ring is a £4 fix rather than a binned machine. Treat the brand's UK service network as part of the price.

## FAQ

**How many bar do I need for a UK patio?**
For typical block paving, Yorkstone or Indian sandstone, 130–145 bar at 6+ L/min is the sweet spot. Below 120 bar you'll fight moss; above 150 bar you risk pitting older sandstone and stripping kiln-dried jointing sand.

**Can I use a pressure washer to clean my car?**
Yes, but drop the pressure or stand back further. Use a vario lance set to a wider fan, keep the nozzle at least 30 cm from the panel, and avoid blasting straight into wheel-arch trims, badges or rubber seals. The Nilfisk's on-handle Power Control is particularly useful here.

**Do I need a hot-water pressure washer?**
For domestic use, no. Hot-water machines are aimed at oil, grease and commercial cleaning. For garden, patio and vehicle work, a cold-water unit with the right detergent is faster, cheaper and lighter.

**Can I run a pressure washer off a water butt?**
Most domestic pressure washers need a positive mains feed to prime the pump and won't reliably draw from a butt without a booster pump. Connect to a garden tap; the saved time and warranty headache is worth it.

**Is an induction motor really worth the extra money?**
If you'll use the machine more than once a month or for sessions longer than 30 minutes, yes. Induction motors are quieter, run cooler and last years longer than universal ones. For a once-a-summer wash, a universal motor is fine.
